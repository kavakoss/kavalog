Coffee isn't just "coffee." There are dozens of varieties, each with distinct flavor characteristics. Here's a quick guide to the most important ones.

---

## Arabica (*Coffea arabica*)

The king of specialty coffee. ~60-70% of global production. Grown at higher altitudes, more delicate, more complex flavor.

| Variety | Flavor Profile | Notable Origins |
|---|---|---|
| **Typica** | Clean, sweet, balanced | Jamaica Blue Mountain, Kona |
| **Bourbon** | Sweet, complex, fruity | Rwanda, Burundi, El Salvador |
| **Gesha / Geisha** | Floral, jasmine, tea-like | Panama, Colombia, Ethiopia |
| **SL-28 / SL-34** | Blackcurrant, juicy, winey | Kenya |
| **Caturra** | Bright, citric, clean | Colombia, Costa Rica |
| **Pacas** | Balanced, chocolate, nutty | El Salvador |
| **Ethiopian Heirloom** | Floral, berry, complex | Ethiopia (Yirgacheffe, Sidamo, Guji) |

---

## Robusta (*Coffea canephora*)

Higher caffeine (~2x Arabica), more bitter, earthy, and harsh. Used in espresso blends for crema and in instant coffee. Grown at lower altitudes. Not typically found in specialty coffee, but high-grade Robusta is gaining respect in some circles.

---

## Processing Methods

How the coffee cherry is processed dramatically affects flavor:

| Method | Process | Flavor Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Washed** | Fruit removed before drying | Clean, bright, transparent |
| **Natural** | Dried with fruit intact | Fruity, funky, heavy body |
| **Honey** | Some mucilage left on | Sweet, syrupy, balanced |
| **Anaerobic** | Fermented without oxygen | Wild, intense, tropical |
