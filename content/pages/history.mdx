---
title: "A Brief History of Coffee"
---

Coffee's journey began in Ethiopia, where legend tells of a goat herder named Kaldi who noticed his goats becoming energetic after eating red berries from a certain bush. From those Ethiopian highlands, coffee traveled to Yemen, where Sufi monks brewed it to stay awake during nighttime prayers.

By the 15th century, coffee had reached Mecca and Medina, and soon spread across the Ottoman Empire. The world's first coffee houses opened in Constantinople (modern-day Istanbul) in the 16th century — places where people gathered to drink, converse, and share ideas.

From the Middle East, coffee reached Europe through Venetian trade routes in the 17th century. Coffee houses became centers of intellectual exchange — so much so that they were sometimes called "penny universities" because for the price of a cup, you could join stimulating conversations.

The Dutch brought coffee plants to Java (Indonesia) in the late 1600s, breaking the Arab monopoly. From there, coffee spread to the Caribbean, Central and South America, and eventually around the globe.

---

## The Three Waves of Coffee

### First Wave (1800s–1960s)
Mass production and consumption. Coffee became a commodity — affordable and accessible, but quality took a back seat. Think Folgers and Maxwell House.

### Second Wave (1970s–2000)
The rise of espresso culture and the coffee shop as a "third place." Starbucks popularized terms like "latte" and "cappuccino." Origin started to matter.

### Third Wave (2000s–Present)
Coffee as an artisanal product — like wine. Roasters highlight single origins, processing methods, and roast profiles. The barista became a craftsperson. This is the wave we're riding now.
