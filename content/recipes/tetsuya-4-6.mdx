## Overview

The **4:6 Method** was developed by **Tetsuya Kasuya**, winner of the 2016 World Brewers Cup. The core idea: split your brew water into 40% (controls sweetness & acidity) and 60% (controls strength). By adjusting the size and timing of each pour, you can shape the flavor profile without changing grind size.

---

## The Formula

- **First 40%** (first two pours) — controls the balance between sweetness and acidity
  - More water in the first pour = sweeter cup
  - Less water in the first pour = brighter, more acidic cup
- **Remaining 60%** (divided into equal pours) — controls strength
  - Fewer pours with larger amounts = weaker body
  - More pours with smaller amounts = stronger, heavier body

## Recipe

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Coffee | **20g** |
| Water | **300ml** (1:15) |
| Temperature | **92–93°C** |
| Grind (X‑Ultra) | **`2.4.0` – `3.0.0`** |
| Grind (K‑Ultra) | **100 – 110 clicks** |
| Filter | Hario V60 paper |

### Pour Schedule

```
0:00  — 40ml — bloom, gentle swirl, wait 45s
0:45  — 60ml (total 100ml — 40% complete)
1:30  — 45ml (total 145ml)
2:15  — 45ml (total 190ml)
3:00  — 45ml (total 235ml)
3:45  — 65ml (total 300ml)

Target drawdown: 3:30 – 4:00
```

### Tips

- **Grind calibration:** If brew finishes before 3:30, grind 3–5 clicks finer. If over 4:00, grind coarser.
- **Pouring:** Pour gently in the center — avoid hitting the paper filter.
- **No stirring:** Swirl only during bloom. Agitation creates bitterness with this method.
